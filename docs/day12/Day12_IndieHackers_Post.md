# Day 12 — Indie Hackers post (the one worth publishing this week)

**Prepared:** 26 Oct 2026 · **Target:** Indie Hackers · **Publish:** Wed 28 Oct, 09:00 ET

*This is the highest-return item in the Day 12 folder, and it is not about the product. It is the directory research, written for people who are about to waste the same afternoon.*

> **Before you post:** every fact below is checked against the live pages on 26 Oct 2026, and the research is yours — you commissioned it, you can stand behind every line. But the sentences should be yours. Change anything that is not true in your voice (“this week”, “an afternoon”) and add the one detail only you know: why you started looking. Indie Hackers doesn't ban drafted text the way HN does, but a post that reads like it was written by a committee gets one polite upvote and no replies.

---

## 1. Why this post and not a launch post

A launch post on IH gets a handful of polite upvotes from other people launching things. This post gets replies, because it does a job for the reader: it saves them an afternoon and it contradicts advice they have already been given.

It also happens to be the only post in the folder that is useful to someone who never visits the site — which is the only reliable reason anything travels on IH, Reddit or X.

Evidence that the genre works there, from the IH front page on 26 Oct 2026: a thread titled *“I submitted a small tools site to ~40 directories in 2 weeks. Here's what was actually free.”* Same shape, different angle. Ours has the numbers and the inconvenient half.

## 3. The post

```
I spent an afternoon doing something unglamorous: reading the pricing page of
every startup directory that the "submit your product to these 30 sites" posts
recommend. Not skimming: reading each free-tier line, then opening every submission URL in
a browser instead of trusting the list.

Four of the seven weren't what the lists said. Here's what I found.

**Why bother.** I run a free browser-tools site: no signup, no revenue yet.
Directories are the only link-building I can afford, so it was worth an afternoon
to find out which ones are real.

**The seven, as they actually are:**

- **Product Hunt** — free, no badge, no queue. The link is nofollow. Its value is
  the day's traffic and the brand search afterwards, not the backlink.

- **AlternativeTo** — the strongest intent of the seven, because people search
  "X alternative" with a tool already in mind. But the submit URL most guides
  give you is a 404 now, and there is no add-app control at all until you have an
  account. Sign in first, then find it.

- **SaaSHub** — free submission, with categories broad enough for a tools site. The URL most lists give is their own directory of 110 submission
  sites, not the form. The form lives at /services/submit.

- **tools.cafe** — free, with a catch: your listing comes with a badge snippet you
  are required to place on your own site before it goes live. Queue when I
  checked: 30 days. Dofollow once the badge is verified.

- **Launchstag** — badge model, dofollow, claim-then-verify. The badge is the
  price of the listing.

- **SaaSCity** — the free tier is dofollow, but there is no map placement, a badge
  is required, and the review queue is around a year. Paid tiers skip it.

- **LaunchBuff** — the one that surprised me. The free tier is a **nofollow** link,
  requires their badge, and free tournament entry is paused because, in their
  words, "our free queue has grown to ~1 year". Dofollow starts at $19.

**Three things worth knowing.**

1. **"Free dofollow directory" usually means one of three things:** free and
   dofollow but a year-long queue; free and dofollow after you put their badge on
   your site; or free and nofollow. Of the seven, exactly one is free, dofollow
   and measured in weeks: tools.cafe.

2. **A dead submit URL is the most common failure in these lists.** Two of the
   seven had a URL that no longer does what the list says. One is a 404 in a
   browser. The other returns a perfectly healthy 200 and serves a completely
   different page. If you're working through a listicle, open each URL and read
   the title — a 200 proves something is there, not that it's the thing you want.

3. **Three of the seven are one company.** tools.cafe's footer lists Launchstag
   and Launchbuff as partner sites. That doesn't make them bad — it does mean
   seven "independent" submissions are five businesses, so sequence by queue
   length rather than by list order.

**What I decided.** Free tiers only. The $19 dofollow link is cheap and I'm not
buying it: paying for a link that passes PageRank is a link scheme whether the
seller calls the fee a listing charge or not, and a site that writes about SEO
folklore can't then buy links. That's not a moral position, it's a consistency one.

Order, by queue length: tools.cafe (30 days), then SaaSHub and AlternativeTo (no
queue), then Launchstag (badge already in my footer), then SaaSCity (submit and
forget for a year). Product Hunt is a launch, not a submission. LaunchBuff I
skipped.

**What I'd tell someone starting today.** Do the no-queue ones first — they cost
an afternoon and pay out immediately. Submit to the year-long queues anyway,
because the cost is ten minutes and the alternative is nothing. And check the link
attribute before the domain rating: a DR 74 nofollow is worth less than a DR 30
dofollow, and half these lists only quote the first number.

If you've done this recently and found one I missed, tell me — I'm keeping the
list current.

Disclosure: I build tools4market.online, which is what sent me down this path.
No affiliate links, and nothing in this post is paid for.
```

**Word count:** 707. That is on the long side for IH but well within what performs there, because it is skimmable — bold lead-ins, short list items, no wall of text.

## 4. Posting mechanics

1. **Post it as a normal post**, not through the Products DB. The Products DB entry is a separate, quieter action you can do later; the post is the thing that gets read.
2. **Morning, midweek.** Wednesday 09:00 ET. A weekend post on a B2B-adjacent site is wasted.
3. **No link in the body except the disclosure line.** One link at the end, after the value. If people want the site, they will click the disclosure.
4. **Reply to every comment for the first four hours.** This is the whole difference between a post and a conversation, and IH rewards the second.
5. **Do not cross-post the identical text to Reddit.** The r/SideProject version in `Day12_Social_Posts.md` is a build story, not this. Same facts, different post — otherwise it reads as syndication and both get ignored.

## 5. Likely replies, and honest answers

**"What's the DR of these directories? You didn't mention it."**
```
I left DR out on purpose. Three of the seven quote their own DR on the pricing
page, and a directory quoting its own authority score is the same as a restaurant
quoting its own review. The link attribute and the queue length are checkable
facts; the DR number is a marketing claim.
```
*(This is also true: tools.cafe and LaunchBuff both quote DR figures on their own pricing pages.)*

**"You're just advertising your site."**
```
Partly, yes — that's why it's at the bottom with a disclosure rather than at the
top. The table is useful whether or not you ever open my site. If it isn't, tell
me which line is wrong and I'll fix it.
```

**"G2 / Capterra / GetApp are missing."**
```
Deliberately. They rank on verified customer reviews, and a free tools site with
no accounts has no way to collect them — the listing would sit empty and unranked.
If you have an account-based product, they belong on the list and this post
doesn't apply to you.
```

**"Just pay the $19, it's nothing."**
```
It's not the $19, it's the signal. A paid dofollow link is a link scheme under
Google's policy regardless of what the seller calls it, and I'd rather have five
free links I can defend than six I have to explain.
```

## 6. What not to do

- **Don't** put the URL in the title, the first line, or a reply to every comment. One link, at the end, disclosed.
- **Don't** claim "I submitted to all seven" — you haven't yet, and the post is about the research, not the submissions. The wording above is "I checked", which is true.
- **Don't** name the competitor directories as villains. The post is about listicles and unverified advice, not about the directories, three of which are run by people who might read it.
- **Don't** add a call to action. There is no ask in this post and that is why it works.
